    ⚙️ Under the Hood

    How Our AI Detects Fake News

    Our system uses a rigorous NLP pipeline and multiple machine learning models to analyze text at a deep level.

    01

    Text Preprocessing

    The article is cleaned by removing URLs, special characters, stopwords and noise. Text is lemmatized and normalized so the model focuses on meaningful content, not formatting.

    NLTK · Lemmatization · Regex
    02

    Feature Extraction

    Using TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency) with up to 50,000 features and bigrams, the text is converted into a numerical vector capturing word importance.

    TF-IDF · 50K Features · Bigrams
    03

    Multi-Model Classification

    Three models — Logistic Regression, Passive Aggressive Classifier, and Linear SVC — compete during training. The best performing model is automatically selected.

    Logistic Regression · PA · LinearSVC
    04

    Explainable AI

    We don't just give a verdict — we show which keywords influenced the prediction the most, so you can understand why the model made its decision.

    Coefficient Analysis · XAI · Transparency

    🛡️ How to Spot Fake News Yourself

    1

    Check the source. Is it a reputable news outlet? Look at the domain name carefully.

    2

    Read beyond the headline. Clickbait headlines are designed to provoke emotion, not inform.

    3

    Verify with other sources. If only one site is reporting it, be skeptical.

    4

    Check the date. Old stories are often reshared as if new to create confusion.

    5

    Look for evidence. Real news cites sources, data, and expert quotes.

    6

    Check your bias. We all have biases — be extra critical of news that confirms what you already believe.
